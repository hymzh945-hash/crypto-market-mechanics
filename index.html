What is inside

The book is not a strategy guide and it does not make price calls. It is an accounting of the machine — who pays whom, when, and under what contract terms.

  • Listing economics. A listing is not a market event. It is a three-party arrangement between the project, the exchange, and the market maker. Each one optimizes something different, and none of them optimizes the price you paid.
  • Maker loans and distribution. Typical loan terms run 2%–5% of supply. The optimal strategy is arithmetic once you see the loan: sell the borrowed tokens into the opening liquidity, buy back lower, return the loan, keep the difference.
  • Unlock schedules. Cliff and linear unlocks release supply on fixed dates. Every unlock is a supply shock, and the effect concentrates in the days around the event.
  • Fee tiers. Exchange fees are charged on volume, not on profit. That single asymmetry explains most of what people call "bad luck".
  • The honest sample. Twelve newly listed perpetuals, measured over 90 days: median drawdown from the first-week high was −54%, but "the open is the peak" only happened in 33% of cases. TIA ran +648% and WIF +640% over the same window. A book that hides those is selling you a story.
